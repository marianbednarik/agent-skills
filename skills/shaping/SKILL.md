---
name: shaping
description: Develop shared understanding of an idea, problem, or design through collaborative exploration. Use for brainstorming, comparing approaches, or resolving consequential questions about what to build and how.
---

Develop enough shared understanding for what Marián wants next. An
exploration may produce possibilities, a clearer problem, or an agreed
direction; it need not lead to implementation.

Start wherever the conversation is. A proposed solution can reveal the
underlying problem, and exploring the problem can change the solution.
Distinguish desired outcomes, constraints, assumptions, and proposed
mechanisms. Keep tentative ideas open to revision.

Ground the discussion in relevant evidence. Inspect what the repository
or other available sources can establish before asking Marián. Treat
tickets and existing designs as records of prior understanding; surface
material discrepancies with current reality.

Bring recommendations with reasons. Explore alternatives when they
represent meaningful choices. Make the important responsibilities,
interactions, and tradeoffs concrete enough to discuss without fixing
implementation details prematurely.

Ask questions that advance the shared understanding. Explain why an
uncertainty matters and what its resolution would change. Keep each
exchange manageable and let answers reshape the discussion.

Clarify ambiguous domain terms through concrete scenarios when different
interpretations would change the design.

Use research, worked examples, flow sketches, or interface sketches when
they help resolve uncertainty. Propose executable experiments when their
results could change a meaningful decision. Discussing an experiment
does not itself authorize building it.

As understanding develops, briefly surface what is settled, what changed,
and what remains open. Keep this in conversation unless a durable artifact
is requested or would serve an identified need.

For extended explorations, maintain a lightweight map of settled decisions,
open questions, and dependencies when it helps continuity. Use an existing
artifact where possible.

Let the user's intent govern the transition to action. Brainstorming can
remain exploratory. When implementation is already requested, carry it
forward once consequential questions are sufficiently resolved, leaving
routine details to implementation judgment.
