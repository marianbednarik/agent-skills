description: >
  Design, implement, redesign, polish, or visually review user-facing frontend UI
  when product and visual judgment materially affect the result. Use for screens,
  components, flows, responsive behavior, interaction states, copy, and visual
  quality. Do not use for purely mechanical frontend changes with no meaningful
  effect on the user experience.
---

# Frontend Design

Make the interface feel intentional for this product, its users, and the surface
being designed.

Before choosing a direction, inspect the context that should shape it:

- durable product, design, language, and engineering guidance when present
- neighboring screens and established interaction patterns
- existing components, tokens, typography, icons, imagery, and other assets

Continue with clearly stated assumptions when useful context is missing and the
task can still move forward. Do not create or refresh project context documents
from this skill.

Understand the user-visible job, the audience and usage context, and whether the
surface primarily supports repeated product work or expresses a brand. Product
interfaces usually prioritize clarity, efficiency, predictable interaction, and
complete states. Brand surfaces can place more weight on composition, emotion,
imagery, and a distinctive point of view. Treat this as design context, not a
fixed style prescription.

Work from the project inward. Preserve its visual and interaction language unless
the task provides a reason to evolve it. Avoid generic defaults and decorative
choices that are disconnected from the product, but do not substitute a universal
aesthetic for project-specific judgment.

Cover the states, content variation, input methods, and viewports that materially
affect the experience. Prioritize real failure modes over exhaustive checklist
completion.

Inspect the rendered result before handoff whenever practical. Use available
browser or app tools, or work from screenshots when direct inspection is not
reliable. Check hierarchy, spacing, alignment, typography, overflow,
responsiveness, interaction feedback, focus, contrast, state coherence, and
anything that feels generic or accidental. Fix visible problems and repeat the
inspection until the result is ready.

When reviewing rather than implementing, distinguish functional or usability
problems from subjective preferences. Prioritize findings by their effect on the
user-visible job and recommend a coherent direction.

If rendered inspection is not possible, state exactly what was not visually
verified. End with a short account of the chosen direction, the states or
viewports inspected, and any remaining visual risk.
