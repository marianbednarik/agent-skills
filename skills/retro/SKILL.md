---
name: retro
description: Review a work session for useful discoveries and friction in the agent's environment, and propose improvements for future sessions. Use only when the user requests a retro or session feedback.
---

Help Marián improve the environment future agents work in: skills, guidance, tools, setup, and information access. Treat the retro as a discussion of worthwhile improvements, not authorization to apply every finding.

Use the current conversation unless another session is specified. Ground observations in available messages, actions, and results. Make important history gaps clear; investigate further only when needed to support or resolve a consequential finding.

Look for consequential friction and useful discoveries: user corrections, stale or missing guidance, unnecessary work, skill misfires, review gaps, difficult information access, and costly tool use. Preserve successful methods when they offer a concrete benefit to future work, including non-obvious site or tool workflows learned during the session.

Distinguish a guidance problem from an execution mistake. Before proposing new instructions, check whether existing guidance already addressed the situation and whether it was available, clear, and followed. An isolated mistake does not automatically justify a permanent rule.

Recommend the smallest useful improvement. Consider correcting or removing guidance, improving discovery, fixing tooling, adding a worthwhile automated check, or preserving a successful procedure. Weigh likely reuse and rediscovery cost against the burden of maintaining and reading what is saved. Prefer a tool or check when it usefully removes repeated work or catches a mechanical error; more prose is not the default remedy.

Keep each improvement with its appropriate owner and one authoritative home:

- Durable project intent and decisions belong in existing project context. Use project-context when establishing or refreshing agreed understanding.
- Project-specific workflow and setup belong in existing documentation, tooling, or a project-local skill. Choose a skill when a recurring procedure benefits from task-specific discovery; retain the conditions and result checks needed to reuse it.
- Personal guidance or a skill shared across projects requires evidence that the lesson applies at that broader scope.

For external limitations, separate the observed failure from its suspected cause and distinguish a local mitigation from a fix owned by the provider. Surface significant friction even when no local fix is available; a concise acknowledgment can be sufficient. Do not turn an unexplained failure into a permanent avoidance rule.

Present worthwhile findings in conversation, ordered by expected benefit. Give enough evidence and concrete detail for Marián to judge what happened, why it mattered, what could improve, and where the change belongs. Include uncertainty that affects the choice. Recommend a direction and discuss alternatives when their tradeoffs matter; avoid a session replay or a full implementation plan for each idea.

Distinguish already-agreed intent from a new preference or policy the session suggests. Apply improvements within the user's authorized update scope; otherwise keep them as proposals. Once a proposal is agreed, carry it through without repeated approval for routine details. Report what changed and what remains proposed.

Reuse existing verification evidence. Surface discovered unfinished implementation work explicitly and handle it according to the authorized task scope. A retro may conclude that no persistent changes are warranted. Finish when useful lessons and proposals are clear and authorized improvements are completed, or their blockers are explained.
