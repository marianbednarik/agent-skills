# Personal skill stack review

Research date: 2026-09-27. Scope: the six canonical personal skills under `skills/`, the project-local `.agents/skills/rethink-skill`, and their relationship to `context/AGENTS.md` and repository `AGENTS.md`. Bundled product skills are complementary tools, not part of the authored set assessed for removal or revision. This is a stack review, not a revision of one runtime skill. Marián reports no specific failure; excessive ceremony and overlapping advice are concerns to assess, not observed incidents. No skill or global instruction was changed.

This records the stack at the time of review. The subsequent [codebase audit redesign](repo-health-audit-reference-sheet.md) renamed `repo-health-audit` to `codebase-audit` and removed its mandatory HTML report.

## Baseline and verdict

The stack has a coherent lifecycle. `shaping` explores consequential uncertainty; ordinary agent judgment implements, diagnoses, and plans; `project-context` preserves agreed durable intent; `check-work` assesses a specific change; `repo-health-audit` examines accumulated structural health; `retro` examines a session and proposes environment improvements; `writing-for-agents` governs the expression of agent-focused documentation; local `rethink-skill` performs deeper, evidence-seeking reassessment of a particular skill. This is an ownership map, not a mandatory chain of invocations.

Keep the current seven authored skills for now. Their boundaries are mostly explicit, and the two conspicuous absences, `plan-work` and `diagnose`, are deliberate retirements rather than unfilled slots. The strongest risk is cumulative guidance and invocation friction: a task may activate several compatible skills, or load a broad one where ordinary judgment would suffice. No current evidence demonstrates that this is happening often enough to justify merging or removing an active skill. The next useful improvement is observational: inspect ordinary runs for skill selection, user effort, duplicate work, and missed outcomes before adding instructions.

The live `~/.codex/AGENTS.md` and canonical `context/AGENTS.md` were byte-identical on this review date. Canonical `skills/` directories are symlinked into `~/.agents/skills/`; local `rethink-skill` is discovered only for this repository. Root `AGENTS.md` explicitly says stored skills are authored material, not instructions to run merely because they are in the repository. `context/AGENTS.md` contains 782 whitespace-delimited words, root `AGENTS.md` 202, and active skill bodies range from 344 to 536 words, except the local rethink procedure at 1,345 words. Body size matters after loading; catalog descriptions matter before loading. These counts alone do not measure cost.

| Capability | Owner and boundary | Why it earns a distinct place | Watch for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Explore an open choice | [`shaping`](../skills/shaping/SKILL.md) | Contributes alternatives and visible reasoning; discussion need not become implementation or an artifact. | Applying deep exploration to a straightforward request after direction is already clear. |
| Preserve settled project intent | [`project-context`](../skills/project-context/SKILL.md) | Owns what belongs in the authoritative, discoverable project record and distinguishes intent from inference or current implementation. | Turning every decision into a new document or reading all context for a small edit. |

| Review a specific change | [`check-work`](../skills/check-work/SKILL.md) | Checks agreed outcome, affected behavior, and maintainability; review-only scope does not authorize edits. | Repeating valid verification or escalating every small change into another reviewer round. |
| Assess accumulated code health | [`repo-health-audit`](../skills/repo-health-audit/SKILL.md) | Separates structural maintenance decisions from review of a completed diff or diagnosis of a bug. | Mandatory external HTML for a substantive audit may cost more than its decision value in some small projects. |
| Learn from a work session | [`retro`](../skills/retro/SKILL.md) | Request-only; routes concrete environment lessons without applying every proposal. | Converting isolated execution mistakes into new permanent rules. |
| Reassess a skill | [local `rethink-skill`](../.agents/skills/rethink-skill/SKILL.md) | Specialized research, scenario comparison, and baseline preservation for a substantial skill decision. | Using its full research-and-eval process for routine wording corrections or all skill-adjacent questions. |

`project-context` and `writing-for-agents` are complementary: the former chooses the durable content and home, the latter edits its expression. `shaping` and `rethink-skill` share exploration, but the latter adds a narrow target and evidence protocol. `check-work` reviews changed work; `repo-health-audit` looks for existing structural debt. `retro` can identify a skill problem but does not itself authorize a redesign. These are real intersections of use, not contradictory instructions.

The surrounding product skills sit at different layers. `skill-creator` handles new skill packaging and structural validation, while `writing-for-agents` shapes the text and local `rethink-skill` tests whether a substantial revision is warranted. File-format and app skills such as documents, spreadsheets, sites, image generation, and visualization own their media or tool workflows; they can coexist with `shaping` or `check-work` on a relevant task. The available catalog is larger than the seven authored skills, so a natural-request activation check should include the full catalog visible to the agent, not just this repository's entries. This review did not assess the internal quality of bundled vendor skills.

## Scenario traces and handoffs

These are analytical walkthroughs of the written guidance, not executed tests of current automatic discovery.

| Ordinary request | Useful route and observable result | Failure that would change the verdict |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Fix issue #14” with a clear issue and relevant project context | Ordinary investigation and implementation; `project-context` updates a durable decision only if it changed; `check-work` reuses targeted verification at completion. | A shaping interview or planning artifact delays the fix; review repeats tests without a new concern; lasting intent silently diverges. |
| “Help me choose a search architecture” | `shaping` compares plausible approaches, assumptions, and a useful next experiment; after agreement, `project-context` preserves the decision if future work needs it. | Tentative interest is written as settled architecture or a complete backlog is produced before uncertainty is resolved. |
| “Audit this repository for maintenance opportunities” | `repo-health-audit` samples hotspots and reports decisions, evidence, and confidence; broad audit can justify a visual report. | A small prototype incurs a large report whose format adds no understanding, or the audit edits code without remediation authorization. |
| “Review this PR” | `check-work` establishes the diff and requirements, follows affected behavior, gives evidence-based findings, and stops when adequately checked. | `repo-health-audit` expands to unrelated debt or review-only scope turns into edits. |
| “Why did this session go badly?” | `retro` separates execution failure from guidance/tooling failure and proposes the smallest useful intervention. A substantial skill concern can later use `rethink-skill`. | Every correction creates another standing rule or a tentative proposal is applied as policy. |
| “Tighten this skill's wording” versus “Rethink whether this skill belongs” | Routine edit uses writing and validation judgment; substantial reassessment uses `rethink-skill` and a research sheet. | The full rethink protocol runs for a small copy edit, or a consequential redesign ships without comparing the baseline. |

Prior repository probes already address part of the case for removal. [`plan-work` research](plan-work-reference-sheet.md) found competent results with and without its skill and retired it while carrying context continuity into standing guidance and `project-context`. [`diagnose` research](diagnose-reference-sheet.md) found similar outcomes across two small runnable cases and retired that skill. [`writing-for-agents` research](writing-for-agents-reference-sheet.md) observed competent documentation without the writing skill on one fixture, while Marián reported a recurring real authoring problem and chose to retain the focused skill. Those tests are useful but small; most assigned bodies directly and did not independently observe automatic discovery or combined activation across a whole workflow. They justify neither blanket retention nor blanket removal.

## Options, recommendation, and open questions

1. **Keep the stack and observe real use (recommended).** Preserve the clear ownership map and recent retirements. Inspect a few ordinary sessions for actual skill reads, duplicate work, unnecessary questions, context updates, and missed decisions. Compare with no-skill or changed-guidance runs when a concrete intervention is proposed.
2. **Merge or remove more skills now.** Reduces catalog and instruction burden, but the plausible targets have distinct ownership or documented personal preference. With no failure case, this risks losing a useful boundary for a speculative token saving.
3. **Add a missing coding workflow skill.** A plan/debug/implementation skill could provide repeatability, but earlier matched cases show competent ordinary behavior and no recurring gap. Favor project-specific runbooks or executable checks if a repeated, concrete operation emerges.

The narrowest candidate for future revision is the external HTML default in `repo-health-audit`, because it is a compulsory output decision that may not suit every substantive prototype audit. Test whether actual audits benefit from it before revising. The second area to observe is whether `check-work` invokes a fresh review too often despite its reuse and stopping criteria. Do not add a universal skill order or a meta-router: the current distinctions can be decided from task intent, and another layer would itself require selection and maintenance.

Open questions are practical rather than textual: how often do natural requests activate the intended skill, does overlapping activation cause meaningful extra work, does a durable decision remain discoverable in a later task, and which report or review actions Marián actually finds worth their latency? Real session traces and a few representative tasks are better evidence than more hypothetical wording. No stack-level behavioral run was executed for this review; the assessment combines source inspection, historical small probes, current primary research, and independent read-only reviews.

Independent checks used fresh contexts and no edits: Luna/high inventoried the canonical skills, Sol/medium assessed workflow handoffs, a second Sol/medium researched current practice and empirical counterweights, and Astra/high assessed the stack without the parent's conclusions. They converged on distinct ownership and an unmeasured cumulative-activation cost. Astra highlighted the HTML mandate and the local rethink procedure as the clearest potential sources of ceremony. These are text and source assessments, not observed selection traces or a behavioral A/B test. Because no candidate skill was written, the rethink procedure's anonymous original-versus-candidate review was inapplicable.
